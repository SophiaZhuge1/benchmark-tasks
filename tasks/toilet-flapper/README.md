# Fit a new toilet flapper

A two-piece toilet tank stands on the table, lid off and empty (the water shut off and the tank flushed, as when a
flapper is changed), its front handle toward the robot and its back against the room wall. The old flapper has been taken off and the new one put into the tank:
it rests on the flush valve's seat, a little forward of its place, with its two rubber ears in front of the overflow
tube's pegs and its bead chain lying on the tank's floor. The chain clip already hangs from the trip lever. The robot
must fit the flapper, hooking both ears over their pegs so that it hangs on them seated on the valve, and set the chain
into the clip at the bead that leaves the right slack, then let go.

What makes it hard: the ears are soft rubber on bendable arms and the pegs stick out sideways from the overflow tube,
so each ear must be drawn outward past its peg's tip, lined up and pushed in along the peg, and the second ear is worked
while the first is held on. The work is two-handed and low in a deep, narrow tank, beside the overflow tube and under the
fill valve's refill tube; the chain is then set at the trip lever on the front wall. The chain must then be set at the right bead: too short and it holds the flapper open (the
toilet runs), too long and the flush is short. The result is checked by working the handle.

Category: Impact on jobs. The workers who change flappers are building maintenance technicians in apartments, hotels
and other buildings, and plumbers. In BLS's May 2023 occupational data, 303,120 Maintenance and Repair Workers, General
(49-9071) worked in real estate (17% of that industry's jobs: the on-site apartment technicians) and 86,170 in hotels;
O*NET 49-9071.00 lists "Assemble, install, or repair ... pipe systems, plumbing ...". Plumbers (O*NET 47-2152.00,
510,600 employed in 2025) have the task "Install pipe assemblies, fittings, valves, appliances such as dishwashers or
water heaters, or fixtures such as sinks or toilets, using hand or power tools." Together that is about 0.9 million workers (the plumbers' count includes pipefitters
and steamfitters). The flapper itself: EPA WaterSense's
Water Efficiency Management Guide for multifamily property owners and managers tells staff to dye-test tank toilets for
worn flappers and "check for a tangled chain in the tank or replace a worn flapper valve"; turnover checklists for
multifamily maintenance staff check "flapper condition" in every unit; the apartment industry's maintenance credential
(NAA CAMT) has a toilets section in its plumbing skill checks. Toilets are 26% of water-related apartment tickets, which
are 21% of all tickets (about 5% of all, in one apartment platform's data), and plumbing is about a quarter of rental
work orders in another's. A running toilet's most common cause is a flapper that doesn't drop properly after flushing,
and the chain's length is critical (This Old House). Sources are listed below.

## Reset (about 2 minutes)

Lift the chain out of the clip (leave the clip in the middle hole). Spread each ear back off its peg, slide the
flapper 15 mm forward on the seat to its pencil mark, ears in front of the pegs, and lay the chain along its tape path
on the floor. Check the refill tube is clipped to the overflow tube's rim and the handle is at rest. Replace the flapper if an
ear tears or its hole has stretched loose; replace the clip if it no longer grips a bead.

## How a real trial is scored

After the robot has let go and stayed at least 3 cm clear for 1 s:

1. Photo: is each ear on its peg (the peg through the ear's hole)?
2. Feeler gauge: does a 0.5 mm feeler enter anywhere between the seal and the seat? If not, the flapper is seated.
3. Steel rule against the lever arm, pressing the handle slowly: the clip rises 10 to 20 mm before the flapper starts
   to lift (the chain's slack; about two beads on this chain).
4. One full press of the handle (the arm turns about 40 degrees): the flapper lifts at least 60 degrees (phone video
   against a mark on the overflow tube 13 cm above the tank floor, the lug's height at 60 degrees) and drops back seated (feeler gauge again).

Score: 1.0 for all four; 0.5 for both ears on and seated otherwise; 0.25 for one ear on (or both on, not seated); 0
otherwise, or if the tank was moved, the flapper is torn, or the robot is within 3 cm of the flapper, chain or lever.

## Run a trial

```bash
inspect-robots run --embodiment yam_arena --policy dream \
  -E task=task.py -E object_jitter=0 -E control_hz=20 -E cam_width=320 -E cam_height=176 \
  --instruction "Fit the new flapper and hook up its chain so that the toilet flushes and doesn't run, then let go." \
  --scorer task_score --max-steps 2400 --store-frames --log-dir runs/dream
```

Scripted reference policy: the same command with `--policy yam_scripted`, which plays `demo()`.
`TASK_DEMO` selects a scripted failure instead: `one_ear` (the right ear is let go in front of its peg: 0.25) or
`slack` (the chain is clipped three beads too long, about 36 mm of slack: 0.5). Offline scorer tests (no simulator): `python test_flapscore.py`.

## The scripted demo

Both hands enter over the rim, spread the ears, align the holes and press each ear against its peg boss. The right hand then parks. The left hand lifts the chain, aligns the selected bead outside the fork mouth and presses along the fork toward the front wall before releasing it. The waypoints are open loop.

## Files

- `task.py`: the scene, the per-step hook (measurements, the ears' peg capture, the chain clip, the scorer's handle
  press) and `demo()`.
- `toiletkit.py`: the tank and the flapper as two PhysX articulations; `EarCatcher` and `ClipCatcher`.
- `flapscore.py`: the scorer as plain functions; `test_flapscore.py` tests each tier and the handle press offline.
- `flapdemo.py`: the scripted reference demo.
- `tankcam.py`: views into the tank for the demo video only (`TASK_TANKCAM=<folder>`): one over the pegs, a close
  view along each peg, and one of the clip; not policy cameras. The demo video cuts to them, enlarged and slowed 3x, when
  the scorer logs each ear onto its peg and the chain into the clip, and ends on a card with the scorer's computed
  handle press from the run's log (the press is not animated).
- `scenekit.py`, `robotpts.py`, `steptime.py`: step and reset hooks, robot sample points for the hands-off check,
  step timing.

## Layout

Both YAM arms stand on 8 cm risers in front of the installed tank. The base centers are 69 cm apart, with their midpoint 39.5 cm in front of the valve seat. The overflow tube and pegs are on the far side. A solid room-wall collider spans 1.5 m, rises 1.2 m above the tank floor and begins 10 mm behind the rear china face. The robot must clear the tank rim on entry and withdrawal. Place the physical tank against a wall or clamp a panel at the same clearance.

## Fidelity: departures from the real trial

Each as "sim (real): reason".

- Peg collider core 3 mm across (real 5.6 mm pin): a thin core lets the rubber ear's hole sit round it without the
  solver fighting a tight rubber fit.
- Ear hole 8.5 mm square, with no stretch force (real: a hole smaller than the peg that the rubber is stretched over;
  the push needed is not measured): an ear is held on its peg only when its hole is on the peg (centre within 4 mm of
  the peg's axis, past the tip) and its tab is pushed home against the peg's boss, the physics engine's contact force
  along the peg being at least 0.5 N for 0.1 s (sampled at 20 Hz); a spring then draws the hole
  round the peg for 0.5 s, and the ear hangs on the core. Lining the hole up is easier than on the real, tight hole.
- Ear spring about 1 N spreads an ear 13 mm (real: an estimate, not measured): each ear is a holed rubber tab on a
  sprung arm; the flapper's seal and cup are rigid.
- Bead mass 1.5 g (real about 0.3 g): heavier beads keep the solver steady. The chain is 21 rigid links of two beads on
  ball joints.
- The lever carries a rigid side-entry fork with a 2 mm wire gap. The selected bead enters from the open end, moves at least 8.1 mm along the fork, and presses its end stop. PhysX contact points on that face are attributed to a bead on the contacting chain link using its surface distance. The same bead must supply at least 0.5 N along the slot on two consecutive control samples before a retaining spring engages. The threshold is an estimate. The spring represents the real clip's retention after seating. Clip flex is omitted.
- Handle press computed (real: the handle is pressed): the scorer's press is computed from the lever's travel (40
  degrees) and the chain's length from the lug to the clipped bead; the flapper lifts once the chain is taut, up to its
  stop against the overflow tube (66 degrees). A chain pulled taut against the flapper's stop is beyond what this
  solver holds steadily, so it is not simulated; the press assumes a straight chain, so a chain caught under the seal
  or looped over a part is not detected by it (the seat check still sees a seal held off its seat). "Drops back
  seated" is taken as seated now with the chain slack at rest.
- No water (real: the water is shut off and the tank flushed for this repair, as here).
