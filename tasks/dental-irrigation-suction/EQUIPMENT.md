# Affordable benchtop equipment and build

Revision for the 15 November 2026 review. This is an **inert dental-training bench**, representing the rinsing/evacuation subtask of dental hygienists and dental assistants. It uses tap/distilled water and a rigid reusable fixture. The equipment below replaces the previous Harvard Pump 11 Elite, KNF vacuum pump, pressure-regulation system and machined acetal parts. No dental chair, compressed-air service, clinical suction unit, dental scaler or medical syringe pump is required.

## Bill of materials

Prices are indicative UK purchasing allowances checked on 15 November 2026, not quotations or evidence of hardware validation. Supplier prices and VAT treatment are stated separately from allowances. The robot, computer and existing phones/cameras are excluded. Budget **GBP 180–240** for the whole bench using existing cameras and local printing; allow another **GBP 20–40** if outsourcing the small print job. Do not buy a printer for this task.

## Assembly without precision machining

1. Open `hardware/fixture.scad` in free OpenSCAD. Export `part="fixture"`, `part="stand"` (two copies), and `part="handle"` (two copies). Alternatively run:
   ```bash
   openscad -o fixture.stl hardware/fixture.scad
   openscad -D 'part="stand"' -o stand.stl hardware/fixture.scad
   openscad -D 'part="handle"' -o handle.stl hardware/fixture.scad
   ```
   The source fixes the 170 x 130 mm base, six 20 mm crowns, 35 mm crown spacing, three 28 x 18 mm floor cells and two 7 x 3 mm connecting gutters. Coordinates are local: add 270 mm to X for tabletop coordinates. Underside supports and sealed channel end walls are fabrication features; they are not additional scored objects. Print the cells open at the top. Seal all wetted seams, smooth the crowns, and check with 6 mL water for leakage into the base. Use contrasting white/pink colours or water-resistant paint. Keep coating thin and measure after finishing.
2. For visible pool depths, replace the two outer long cell walls with 1 mm clear PET strips at the same inner faces y=+/-9 mm, top z=34.5 mm. Measure with a ruler/caliper. Add removable clear 6 mm-wide runoff guides from each target crown's inner edge toward its cell; nominal crown-edge z=37, floor z=31. Guides and end seals are physical water-containment details approximated by the simulator. All their retained water counts in mouth inventory; no drain bypasses the suction inlet.
3. Secure hollow stock tubing along the documented tool centreline using ties and a rigid scrap-plastic support **behind** the tube. Preserve distal OD5/OD9 mm, tip position (0,+/-90,-25) mm and the grip datum (0,0,40) mm relative to each tool origin. Use smooth bends without kinks; measure the assembled envelope. The CAD handle has the simulated external dimensions. Ballast with washers to 75 g including the moving hose; put ballast inside the grip/handle envelope. If supports intrude into a scored clearance or the tool cannot reach the floor, rebuild it before a trial.
4. Put the fixture at (270,0) mm and the two 50 mm-diameter stands at (280,+200) and (280,-200) mm. Stand tops and initial tool origins are z=30 mm. Clamp the base. Support hose slack from above so it does not pull the tools off their stands. Keep pumps, power and electronics outside the spill tray.
5. Feed path: water bottle -> feed pump -> primed silicone hose -> irrigation tool. Recovery path: evacuation inlet -> short clear hose -> recovery pump -> open collecting cup. **Do not seal or evacuate an ordinary cup.** The peristaltic pump moves the liquid inside its tube; no vacuum receiver, gauge or clinical aspirator is needed. Record hose length and holdup.
6. Calibrate the actual assembled system before mounting it in the robot. Keep the simulator's fixed pulse windows (16–21, 24–29, 32–37 seconds). Set feed mean flow to 0.40 mL/s; commissioning allowance is 1.90–2.10 mL per five-second pulse, with measured actual delivery used for physical scoring. Repeat ten pulses, record min/max and start/stop drips. This supersedes the previous expensive-pump +/-0.02 mL procurement requirement. It does **not** relax 1.8 mL/tooth or any scored water limit. A pump that cannot meet the actual scoring and timing requirements is unsuitable even if its nominal rating is sufficient.
7. Adjust the recovery pump toward measured 0.65 mL/s with its inlet submerged. Then test the declared working height, air ingestion, each cell, a dry cell and a raised inlet. Measure how much remains, and all transit delays. A peristaltic pump is a bench evacuation surrogate, not a clinical saliva ejector; its real near-dry recovery is not established by its catalogue flow rate.

## Measurement with ordinary equipment

Use a printed 1 mm grid and close camera framing. At 1080p, frame a 120 mm-wide mouth region across at least 1,000 pixels (about 0.12 mm/pixel); verify the achieved resolution with the grid, including an oblique view for Z. Use diffuse lighting and transparent cell walls. Calibrate visible water levels with known weighed additions. Coverage, guards, the cleanup sequence and rack return are reviewed from timestamped video. The detailed regions and limits remain in `PHYSICAL_PROTOCOL.md`.

For final near-dry inventory, use a needle-free syringe plus preweighed absorbent strips to collect **all** remaining crown/guide/channel water into a small tared cup. Include water in strips; weigh promptly and use a wet/dry blank to bound evaporation and residual collection error. Never try to weigh the >50 g fixture on the pocket scale. A 0.001 g display does not establish 0.001 g accuracy: demonstrate final-volume uncertainty <=0.01 mL before claiming the <=0.05 mL endpoint. Use the same mass method for supplied/recovered/spilled water, accounting for liquid inside the suction tube.

The whole-trial peak still requires video of all pools and crown films, calibrated known-volume reference images, and a supplied-minus-recovered/lost cross-check with hose-delay bounds. A final measuring-cup reading alone is insufficient. If ordinary-camera calibration cannot establish peak <=1.00 mL within uncertainty, label the trial **unverified**; do not assume success. This is the remaining hardware validation dependency, not a requirement to purchase specialist optical equipment.
